# Clitic Pronouns

Inglisce has two ways of writing an object pronoun after its verb. Where the verb offers a
vowel, the pronoun reduces against it, attaches, and an apostrophe marks what was lost.
Everywhere else it stays a free word.

### SUMMARY

- **After a silent -E** — the pronoun reduces and attaches — I see him → `I sihe'm`
- **A present-tense -C verb takes the pre-vowel stem to host it** — I take him → `I tâche'm`
- **Everywhere else** — a free pronoun, written separately: past, third person present, and
  any other consonant-final stem
- **Direct and indirect objects behave alike.** There is no separate dative form.

---

## The Pronouns

| English | Free form | Reduced free form | Clitic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| him | `him` | — | `'m` |
| her | `her` | — | `'r` |
| them | `þem` | `hem` | `'m` |
| you | `you` | `ya` | — |

**The *h* is written but not pronounced.** It records the Middle English form rather than
the modern sound, so the letter is historical and the reduction is phonetic.

**On *them*.** The reduced `hem` is not a shortening of `þem`. It continues Middle English
*hem*, the accusative and dative plural, which the Norse *þeim* displaced. English does the
same when it writes *'em*, which is not a clipping of *them* but the survival of *hem*. So
the two free forms are two different words that came to mean the same thing, and the
reformed spelling keeps both.

**One consequence.** *Him* and *hem* both reduce to `'m`, so the clitic does not distinguish
them. The free forms do.

## The Three Contexts

### 1. A Stem Ending in Silent -E

The verb gives up nothing. The clitic attaches to the **-e** already there.

**Examples:**
I see him → `I sihe'm`
I see her → `I sihe'r`

A /k/ stem written with final **-que** belongs here too. The **-e** is already in place, so
nothing has to change to host the clitic.

### 2. A Present-Tense -C Verb

A verb ending in bare **-c** has no **-e** to offer. To host a clitic it takes the pre-vowel
stem in **ch**, the same one that serves **-ing**, and **-ed** in a weak verb.

**This holds for the present tense only, and not for the third person.** The **-s** would
stand immediately before the apostrophe, and the system does not write that sequence.

The rule is about the **-c**, not about the verb class. A strong verb and a weak one behave
alike.

| Form | Strong | Weak |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| infinitive, present | `taic` | `baic` |
| third person present | `taics` | `baics` |
| past | `tôc` | `bâched` |
| progressive | `tâching` | `bâching` |
| present + him, them | `tâche'm` | `bâche'm` |
| present + her | `tâche'r` | `bâche'r` |

### 3. Every Other Context

Where the stem cannot host a clitic, the pronoun is a free word and nothing joins it to the
verb — no apostrophe, no hyphen. That covers the past (`tôc`), the third person present
(`taics`), and every other consonant-final stem. Two reduced free forms are available here,
`hem` for *them* and `ya` for *you*; `him` and `her` have none.

## Romance Precedent

Only the clitic has a Romance parallel. The free form is ordinary English word order.

| Language | Device | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Catalan | reduced pronoun against a vowel, fuller form otherwise | *porta'l* : *portar-lo* |
| Italian | infinitive drops its final vowel; pronoun written solid | *parlare* → *parlarmi* |
| Spanish | pronoun written solid, stem unchanged | *comer* → *comerlo* |
| Portuguese | infinitive drops its final consonant; stem takes an accent | *amar* → *amá-lo* |
| Romanian | a supporting vowel appears only when the clitic attaches | *văzând* → *văzându-l* |
| French | a letter is restored for the clitic to lean on | *va* → *vas-y* |

Catalan is the nearest parallel: the apostrophe against a vowel, the choice made by what the
stem ends in. Inglisce differs in letting the alternative be a free word rather than an
attached one, which is closer to what French and Spanish do with a finite verb.

**One clitic at a time.** Romance stacks two (Spanish *dármelo*, Italian *dammelo*).
Inglisce does not. *Give* is the only English verb with an obvious use for it, and one
pronoun is enough.

## Teaching Notes

**The rule in one line:** give the pronoun a vowel and it attaches; give it a consonant and
it stands apart.

**Key distinctions:**
- The apostrophe marks only what the pronoun lost, never anything the verb did
- `'m` covers *him* and *þem* alike; the free forms keep them apart
- Only one pronoun attaches at a time
