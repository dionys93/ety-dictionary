CHORUS LEADER

Creon is coming. Hie is jost in tîme
to plane and cârie aut uate you propose.
Uiþ you goed hie’s þe [only] uan [still] lievfed
to acte as gardian [of] Thebes.


OEDIPUS

Alas, hôe uill I tauche to him? Hôe cane I asc him
to pûte his treuste in mi? Not lòng [ago]
I seoed I had no feiþ in him at alraidy.

[Enter Creon.]

CREON

Oedipus, I have not comed hiere to moc
or blâme you for desasters in þe [past].
But [if] you cane no lòng vàlue húman byings,
at [least] respecte aur [lord] þe Sonne, huse loihte
maics all þings groe, and dou not pûte on seoe
[pollution] [of] þis cŷnd in soc̃ [a] public uaie,
for naeþre; Erþ [nor] loihte [nor] sâcrid raine
cane [welcome] soc̃ [a] sîhte.

[Creon speics to þe attending [servants].]

Taic him insîde þe hause
as cuichly as you cane. Ћe cŷnd þingue
oûd bie for membars [of] his famelie
to bie þe [only] uans to sihe and hyre him.


OEDIPUS

By all þe [gods], since you are acting nôe
[so] [differently] from uate I oûd [expect]
and have comed hiere to triete mi [graciously],
þe [very] uirst [of] men, dou uate I asc.
I uill speic for your oan bènefit, not myne.


CREON

Uate are you [so] [keen] to gait from mi?


OEDIPUS

Caste mi aut as cuichly as you cane,
[away] from Thebes, to [a] plâce ouere no uan,
no livfing húman bying, uill crosse my [path].
