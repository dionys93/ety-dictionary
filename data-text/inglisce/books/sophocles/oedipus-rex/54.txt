OEDIPUS

No, no. It’s public gnollage. Loxias
once saied it uas my fâte þat I oûd mârie
my oan maþre and scede my faþre’s blode
uiþ my oan [hands]. Ћat’s uoie, many years ago,
I lievfed my hoame in Corinth. Ћings tourned aut ouel,
but [nonetheless] it guives þe suiet joie
to lôc into þe eais [of] uan’s oan pârents.


MESSENGER

And becase you uere afréid [of] her
you staied [away] from Corinth?


OEDIPUS

And becase I did not uante to bie my faþre’s [killer].


MESSENGER

My [lord], since I comed to maic you happy,
uoie dou I not relieve you [of] þis fiare?


OEDIPUS

You oûd receive from mi [a] [worthy] þâncs.


MESSENGER

Ћat’s rilly uoie I comed—[so] your retourne
máit prouve [a] bènefit to mi bac hoame.


OEDIPUS

But I uill nevre goe bac to my pârents.


MESSENGER

My sone, it is [so] clier you’ve no îdía
uate you are douing . . .


OEDIPUS [interrupting]

Uate dou you mine, oald man?
In þe nâme [of] all þe [gods], telle mi.
