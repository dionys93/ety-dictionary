- ## Old English vowels and what they became

Old English had seven short vowels, seven long vowels, and several diphthongs. Most modern outcomes come from a handful of later changes: lengthening in open syllables and before clusters like ld and nd (13th century and earlier), shortening before other clusters, the Great Vowel Shift (1400–1700), and the split of short u into /ʌ/ and /ʊ/ (17th century).

### Short vowels

| Old English | Usual General American result | Examples | Other outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | /æ/ | *mann* → man | lengthened in open syllables → /eɪ/: *nama* → name, *bacan* → bake |
| æ | /æ/ | *æt* → at, *glæd* → glad | |
| e | /ɛ/ | *bedd* → bed | lengthened in open syllables → /i/: *etan* → eat, *sprecan* → speak |
| i | /ɪ/ | *fisc* → fish | before ld, nd → /aɪ/: *cild* → child, *findan* → find |
| o | /ɑ/ | *god* → god | open syllable → /oʊ/: *hopa* → hope; before ld → /oʊ/: *ald* → old |
| u | /ʌ/ | *sunu* → son | after labials → /ʊ/: *full* → full |
| y | /ɪ/ | *fyllan* → fill, *cyssan* → kiss | dialect fossils: *busy*, *bury* |

### Long vowels

| Old English | GA result | Examples | Other outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ā | /oʊ/ | *stān* → stone, *hām* → home | Northern /eɪ/, as in Scots *stane*; this is how *rād* gave both *road* and *raid* |
| ǣ | /i/ | *dǣl* → deal, *clǣne* → clean, *rǣdan* → read | shortened → /ɛ/: *brǣþ* → breath |
| ē | /i/ | *fēt* → feet, *grēne* → green, *cēpan* → keep | |

| ō | /u/ | *gōs* → goose, *mōna* → moon | shortened → /ʊ/: *gōd* → good, *fōt* → foot; earlier → /ʌ/: *blōd* → blood |

| Old English | GA result | Examples | Other outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
